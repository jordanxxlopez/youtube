## Character and performance

Use the relevant approved PNG as an actual visual reference, following
`../../../characters/teo/CHARACTER_LOCK.md`. Preserve Teo's face, curls, skin
tone, physique, proportions, clothing, emblem, accessories, and palette across
every shot. Natural expressions, turns, walking, gestures, and speaking mouth
and jaw movement are permitted; the character lock does not freeze his pose.

Show purposeful full-body action: grounded steps, believable weight transfer,
hands reaching for and holding props, appropriate eye lines, and reactions
to events and other speakers. Match mouth/jaw motion to the actual dialogue
performance, including pauses, stress, emotion, and rhythm. Keep each speaker's
voice identity and recording character consistent across shots and styles.
Choose the voice/performance workflow before large-scale shot generation;
replacing a voice requires matching or redoing its visible speech animation.

## Staging and continuity

Compose complete environments with Teo physically present in them. Use wide,
medium, and closer shots motivated by action and storytelling. Maintain room
geography, lighting, camera direction, eye lines, scale, prop locations,
hand-to-prop contact, and the character's movement between cuts. Science
demonstrations should be events he can observe, approach, manipulate, or react
to inside that world.

The approved reference includes a warm room and a projected brain. A brain
hologram is allowed as a visible object within the environment. The rejected
approach is the simulation lecture interface: a portrait in a fixed left panel,
slide-like diagram boards, and cutout motion used as the main staging. Do not
recreate that treatment or substitute pasted mouth shapes for the benchmark's
integrated facial performance. Use captions for accessibility without turning
the frame into a lecture slide.
